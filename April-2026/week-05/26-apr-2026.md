Title: Audience-Aware Pre-Registration Forms Pipeline: Five-Phase Execution Closing Consent Design, Nested Routing, Backend Wiring, and a Five-Bug Submission Chain

Objective:
To complete the multi-audience pre-registration forms pipeline on the public marketing site by refining the consent component into a footer-style disclaimer, restructuring routes to nest each audience-specific form under its parent landing page, wiring the backend `/api/contact` and `/api/forms/confirm` routes for audience-aware persistence, adding two missing renderers to the in-house form engine, and verifying the full submission flow against a running development server through Playwright. The secondary goal was to peel back a five-bug submission chain that hid behind a green TypeScript checker and prove that integration testing against a live HTTP path is the only reliable signal for a multi-layered form pipeline. The tertiary goal was to land the work as twenty-two disciplined website commits with zero TypeScript errors at session close, holding to a commit-only mode pending manual verification of the credentialed surfaces.

Work Carried Out:

**Consent Component Refinement from Numbered Section to Footer-Style Disclaimer:** The session opened with a design correction on the pre-registration form's consent block, which had been rendering as a numbered "section 99" with a "Consent" legend heading and was further appearing as a plain text input on the live page. The text-input fall-through was inherited from the prior session and traced to a missing `type: 'consent'` branch in the form engine's render switch, which caused the field to default to `<input type="consent">` that browsers render as a generic text box. The renderer was added, and the design was then taken further by dropping the `number: 99` and `title: 'Consent'` fields from the manifest, introducing a new `compact` prop on the Checkbox primitive at sixteen-pixel visual size and twelve-pixel muted-gray text, and teaching the form engine to detect consent-only sections and render them inside a plain `<div>` rather than a numbered `<fieldset>` with legend. The end result was a small-print checkbox plus a Privacy Policy link sitting between the last form section and the submit button, mirroring the disclaimer aesthetic established by enterprise checkout flows, and was landed across three commits covering the manifest cleanup, the primitive change, and the section-style switch.

**Nested Routing Restructure for Audience-Specific Forms:** The marketing site previously routed audience pre-registration entry points through a flat `/pre-registration/[audience]` namespace, but feedback on the live URL bar drove a restructure where each audience form lives as a leaf under its parent landing page. Three new audience-specific form pages were authored at `/for-businesses/pre-registration`, `/for-non-profits/pre-registration`, and `/for-governments/pre-registration`, each with its own field manifest and audience-tagged form configuration, while the individual `/pre-registration` route remained unchanged because individuals enter directly from the homepage rather than through an audience landing page. Each audience landing page's call-to-action was repointed to the nested form. The reasoning followed the navigation-hierarchy pattern used by enterprise platforms such as Stripe and Plaid, where the context-bearing parent route gives the participant location awareness in the URL bar and the form is treated as a child surface rather than a top-level entry point. The restructure landed across the three new page commits, three call-to-action repoint commits, and one `refactor(routing)` commit that consolidated the move.

**Backend Pipeline Wiring for Audience-Aware Persistence:** With the forms rendering correctly the next phase traced the full submission pipeline from form payload through the contact API, the confirmation token issuance, the confirm endpoint, and the Sheets append, and identified six gaps that each became a focused commit. The form engine was concatenating all field values into a single `message` string but not sending individual key-value pairs, which meant the API could not store `company`, `country`, or `industry` as separate columns in the Sheets backend; this was corrected by packing all string field values into a `metadata` map alongside the existing message. The `/api/contact` route was updated to validate the new `audience` field against the canonical `AUDIENCES` tuple, enforce server-side consent presence by returning a four hundred status when `version` or `acceptedAt` was missing on a pre-registration submission, and inject both the audience and the consent metadata into the confirmation token payload. The confirm route's `buildSheetRow` for pre-registration was widened from six columns (timestamp, name, email, UTM, environment, message) to sixteen columns covering audience, phone, company, position, country, industry, website, procurement interest, and consent metadata, allowing the Sheets API to serve as a structured operational store rather than a flat audit log. Two further commits removed all eight occurrences of the banned brand term from the codebase across email templates, page metadata, the homepage, the confirmation panel, and the individuals page, replacing it consistently with the brand-approved phrasing.

**Audience-Aware Confirmation and Notification Email Templating:** The Resend integration previously dispatched the same generic confirmation copy for every audience, and the team-side notification email was a flat name-plus-email-plus-message dump that lost all of the structured organisation data. The `buildConfirmationEmail` helper was updated to accept an `audience` parameter and produce audience-specific subject lines and body copy, branching the user-facing language so a Business pre-registration receives different framing than a Non-Profit or Government one. The notification email path was enhanced to surface organisation, role, country, and industry when present in the payload, prefix the subject with a brand tag, and add an audience tag through the Resend message envelope so internal triage and routing can branch on audience without parsing the body. A small but important copy correction was also landed clarifying that pre-registration is an interest form for the early-access mailing list and does not guarantee on-chain registration, allocation, or any unit grant, with the clarification appearing in three places: the confirmation email body, the registry success message, and the email subject which was reworded from "registration" to "interest."

**Five-Bug Submission Chain Surfacing Behind a Green Type Checker:** With the forms rendering and the backend wired the actual submission still failed, and three layered bugs surfaced in sequence with each one only visible after the prior was repaired. The first was a four hundred and three forbidden_origin response triggered by the development server running on port four-five-four-five while the `DEV_ORIGINS` allowlist in the API security module covered only the three thousand range, which was fixed in a two-line addition for both the `localhost` and `127.0.0.1` host forms. The second was a five hundred and three rate_limiter_unavailable response thrown by the Redis-backed limiter because Redis was not running locally; the prior session's migration from an in-memory map to Redis was correct for production but had wired the in-memory fallback only behind a `NODE_ENV === 'test'` check, so the helper was renamed from `isTestEnv()` to `useInMemoryFallback()` and rewritten to return true when the runtime is not production and `REDIS_URL` is unset, preserving the production fail-closed posture while degrading gracefully for development. The third was a four hundred captcha_failed response that turned out to be two distinct bugs stacked one above the other: the registry's `enableCaptcha: false` for pre-registration was being silently overridden because the page component destructured a fixed list of props and missed `enableCaptcha`, causing the form engine to default it to true and render the math captcha; the fix replaced the brittle destructuring with a `{...clientConfig}` spread matching the pattern the three audience pages already used. The deeper layer was server-side: the API enforced the captcha unconditionally whenever `CAPTCHA_SECRET` was set in the environment, treating the client-side `enableCaptcha` flag as a rendering hint with no server-side authority, so even after the client stopped rendering the captcha the server was rejecting the empty captcha answer; the enforcement block was wrapped in a `if (!isPreReg)` guard since pre-registration forms verify humans through email confirmation rather than a math challenge.

**The Type-Checker-Versus-Integration Insight:** The five-bug chain was instructive as a discipline lesson because `npx tsc --noEmit` returned exit zero throughout every one of the five failures. The TypeScript compiler accepted the missing port allowlist, the Redis-only rate limiter path, the destructuring miss, the unconditional captcha enforcement, and the propagation of the empty captcha answer, because none of these are type-level concerns; they are integration-level concerns that only surface when a real HTTP POST runs against a real server, against a real Redis (or its absence), against real environment configuration, against real downstream services. Each layer of the pipeline (origin check, rate limiter, captcha gate, field validation, email delivery) works correctly in isolation and passes its unit-level checks. The interaction between layers under a real request is what creates the failure modes. The lesson was codified as a session-level rule: after any change to a function's return type or a pipeline's execution order, the next step is not "run typecheck" but "POST real data and trace the response."

**Two Missing Form Engine Renderers Built Through Parallel Agent Dispatch:** Two field types declared in the audience manifests had no renderer in the form engine and would have rendered as broken text inputs in production: `type: 'segmented'` for the Government form's procurement interest control, and `type: 'autocomplete'` for the Business form's industry selector. Two parallel agents were dispatched to add both renderers cleanly. The segmented control was implemented as a `role="radiogroup"` container with pill-style buttons, roving tabindex semantics, arrow-key navigation between options, a brand-purple selection border, and full-width spanning through a `sm:col-span-2` class. The autocomplete renderer was modelled on the existing country-field renderer, filtering against a configurable `field.autocompleteOptions` list, closing the dropdown on exact match, and using `onMouseDown` with `e.preventDefault()` to prevent blur-before-click from swallowing the selection. Both agents returned with clean type checks. A small follow-up bug fix was also landed correcting the country dropdown to close after an exact-match selection rather than remaining open, which had been an unrelated paper cut surfaced during the verification pass.

**Playwright End-to-End Verification Across All Four Forms:** With the renderers complete and the five-bug chain repaired, a Playwright headless Chrome run was executed against the development server on port four-five-four-five exercising all four forms. Each form was navigated to its page, all fields were filled with test data including the country autocomplete selection and the Government segmented control, the consent checkbox was clicked, the form was submitted, the success screen reading "Check your email to confirm" was verified, and zero console errors and zero four-hundred or five-hundred class responses were asserted. The Individual form at `/pre-registration` filled three fields plus consent and returned a two-hundred response with a Resend confirmation identifier; the Business form at `/for-businesses/pre-registration` filled nine fields plus consent and returned a confirmed response; the Non-Profit form at `/for-non-profits/pre-registration` filled eight fields plus consent and returned a two-hundred response with a Resend identifier; and the Government form at `/for-governments/pre-registration` filled eight fields plus the segmented control plus consent and returned a confirmed response. All four forms passed. Specific verifications included country autocomplete filtering and exact-match closure, industry autocomplete filtering, segmented control click-to-select rendering, consent checkbox compact footer styling, the submission payload including `audience`, `consent: { version, acceptedAt }`, and all field values in `metadata`, and the API response shape `{ ok: true, confirmationId: "uuid" }` confirming Resend dispatch.

**Admin Dashboard Gap Analysis and Carry-Forward Triage:** A final inspection of the admin submissions page revealed that its `ApplicationFieldsCard` component renders structured field cards only for partner-flow and validator-flow form types, returning null for the pre-registration form type, which meant audience-specific submissions would arrive with their generic top-level fields plus a raw metadata JSON dump rather than a formatted card showing organisation, role, country, and industry. This was scoped as a carry-forward item rather than landed in this session because the fix requires testing against the admin dashboard, which is gated by an authenticated session and falls outside the credential-free verification surface. The carry-forward list was extended to include the admin dashboard integration for audience-aware submission display, opaque UUID tokens with single-use Redis enforcement, per-audience Sheets routing across four separate worksheets, the pending Cloudflare Turnstile widget integration that will eventually replace the math captcha across the remaining forms, a mobile viewport Playwright sweep at three hundred and seventy-five pixels, and per-audience analytics event scaffolding. The credentialed verification surface (confirmation email link click, database write on confirmation, Sheets append, admin dashboard display) was handed off to manual testing with real credentials, and the code held to commit-only mode pending that manual pass before remote push.

Outcome:
The forms pipeline transitioned from infrastructure to product in a single Sunday session, with all four pre-registration forms (Individual, Business, Non-Profit, Government) rendering correctly with audience-appropriate field types, submitting through an audience-aware backend pipeline, dispatching audience-specific confirmation emails through Resend, persisting structured sixteen-column metadata to the Sheets backend including consent records, and showing a consistent "check your email" success flow. Twenty-two website commits landed across consent refinement, three new audience pages, one routing restructure, six backend wiring commits, three security and rate-limiter fixes, two new form engine renderers, the brand-language sweep across eight files, and the captcha double-fix, with zero TypeScript errors at session close, four out of four Playwright passes, and approximately one thousand eight hundred lines added across forms, API, renderers, and section manifests.
