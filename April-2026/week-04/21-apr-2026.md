Title: End-to-End Wireframe Audit Across Seven Phases: 285-Screen Eight-Agent Fleet, 52 Canonical Gaps, and Registry Integrity Reconciliation

Objective:
To execute a deepest-depth end-to-end audit across all seven wireframe phases of the participant, institutional, government, unified admin, command centre, future-modules, and cross-cutting surfaces by dispatching a calibrated eight-agent audit fleet against a shared rubric, consolidating raw findings into a canonical gap registry, and reclassifying carry-forward items by their true structural severity. The secondary goal was to repair the project's cross-document registry integrity, surface persona and decision-drift contradictions visible only across phase boundaries, and produce a prioritised P0 ladder that the next session could execute against without re-deriving severity from raw audit text.

Work Carried Out:

**Audit Scope Establishment and Rubric Authoring:** The session opened with a directive for a full end-to-end audit across all seven wireframe phases totalling 285 screens, with the explicit instruction that depth, scope, and output format were left to the orchestrator's discretion. Rather than dispatch the agent fleet immediately and risk eight differently calibrated audits with no consolidation leverage, a shared rubric document was authored first as the calibration instrument. Seven evaluation categories were defined covering render integrity, structural balance, language-spec compliance, naming and persona consistency, primitive-reuse discipline, locked-decision compliance, and visual anomaly sweep. The rubric pinned forbidden-language patterns (em dashes, the word "user" in participant-facing UI copy, "platform" referring to the protocol, "coin" outside permitted URL exceptions, the canonical currency format, and marketing phrasing), enumerated permanent exceptions covering filenames, database columns, chaincode event fields, and package names, and mandated a uniform output contract so every auditor's report would share the same Markdown structure. The toolchain reference paths to Playwright Chromium were confirmed and embedded in the rubric so each auditor could perform headless browser renders against a known-good binary.

**Eight-Agent Fleet Dispatch and Phase-Specific Briefing:** The fleet was dispatched in a single parallel message rather than sequentially to maximise wall-clock compression and avoid serialisation penalties. Seven phase-specific auditors were briefed against their target HTML files plus phase-specific focus hints, while an eighth cross-document auditor received the supporting Markdown files and was tasked with cross-phase consistency checks that only surface when grep spans every phase artefact at once. Phase 3 and Phase 4 auditors were primed to hunt a specific carry-forward persona-drift hypothesis; the Phase 5 auditor was reminded of a known prior-session bug zone; the Phase 6 auditor was given the expected artboard count of twenty plus one deferred roadmap entry to validate against. Each auditor was instructed to begin its candidate gap numbering at the next free identifier so consolidation could remap collisions afterwards. The fleet ran in background mode with notifications firing as each agent completed.

**Phase-by-Phase Findings and Severity Distribution:** The phase auditors returned in staggered order over roughly thirty minutes of wall-clock time. The cross-cutting phase auditor returned first with fourteen findings and zero P0 issues but exposed a structural surprise: the file shipped every cross-cutting state (consent, session lifecycle, theme, empty states, offline, error pages, settings, support, legal) yet missed five cross-cutting primitives that the remaining phases were silently reimplementing each time, including toast and snackbar, loading skeleton, destructive-confirmation dialog, inline form-validation error, and rate-limit cooldown surfaces. The institutional phase returned twelve findings including a real visual bug where an endorsement-invite primary call-to-action clipped seventeen pixels below the device frame, alongside a decision-drift case where a reviewer note claimed an inline audit-log tab that did not exist in the rendered output. The unified admin phase landed eight findings and definitively located a long-tracked persona-drift carry-forward to a single line on the participant-government phase, simultaneously uncovering that the Direct-queue sample data was type-invariant and showed business and non-profit queues with government-only recipients. The command-centre phase produced fourteen findings including three forbidden-noun copy violations, a permission-matrix overflow of up to thirty pixels horizontally against the available pane width, and mixed fabric-identifier conventions inside a single file. The future-modules phase produced thirteen findings including the fleet's first P0 — velocity-tax band thresholds off by approximately one hundred-fold against the canonical tokenomics specification, with a named participant placed in the wrong band as a direct consequence. The participant-government phase returned twelve findings confirming the persona-drift epicentre as systemic across four named personas on three distinct transfers, with amount drift compounding on top across a single transfer that appeared in three different ledger values across four narrative screens. The participant-wallet phase produced the largest single haul of twenty-nine findings and four P0 issues, headlined by fourteen artboards clipping between twenty-eight and seven hundred eleven pixels below a fixed device frame, with one profile screen clipping ninety-six percent of its content below the fold.

**Cross-Document Registry Integrity Surfacing:** The cross-document auditor returned last and produced the most consequential structural finding of the entire pass: thirty-two findings, eight of them P0, all relating to project-wide registry integrity rather than any single phase's render quality. The open-decisions registry claimed a contiguous numerical range but contained twenty-four missing identifier holes across two distinct gaps; one decision was cited in narrative text but never defined; the gap register used duplicate identifiers across its phase-specific tables, producing seventy-two table rows for seventy monotonic identifiers; nineteen carry-forward items referenced in the phase HTML files and prior work records were never entered into the authoritative carry-forward table; two phases had off-by-one declared-versus-rendered screen counts that crossed over a phase boundary; a single named persona was simultaneously a trusted authoriser in one phase and a frozen sanctions-hit participant in another; the patterns document declared fifteen cross-cutting primitives by names that did not match the actually implemented primitive symbols. The single most painful finding was a sweep of ninety-three em-dash violations across four supporting Markdown files plus seven instances in the index file — every phase HTML was clean while the no-em-dashes hard rule had been silently skipped on every Markdown edit.

**Consolidation, Deduplication, and Canonical Gap Numbering:** The eight auditors collectively produced one hundred thirty-four raw findings with overlapping candidate-gap identifiers across the fleet. A dedicated consolidator agent was spawned with explicit deduplication rules, severity-take-the-higher reconciliation, and source-attribution preservation. The consolidator collapsed multiple-angle duplicates (where the same defect surfaced from a phase auditor and the cross-document auditor independently) and reassigned canonical monotonic identifiers from the next free integer onward. The output was a four-hundred-thirty-five-line canonical audit report carrying a totals table, a cross-phase themes section, a priority action list, and a complete gap-numbering remapping, plus an appended fifteenth section to the gap-register file containing fifty-two canonical gaps each carrying identifier, phase, screen or location, description, severity, and source-audit attribution. Final aggregate totals stabilised at two hundred eighty-five declared screens, two hundred eighty-four actually rendered (a net minus-one from the off-by-one phase swap), one hundred thirty-four raw findings, fifty-two canonical gaps, thirteen P0 blockers, sixty-three P1 should-fix items, and fifty-eight P2 polish items.

**Carry-Forward Prioritisation and Severity Reclassification:** The audit's most significant operational outcome was a reclassification of the previously tracked persona-drift carry-forward from a P2 polish task to a P0 structural blocker. The original carry-forward had treated the issue as a single-row narrative inconsistency requiring a one-line edit. The audit revealed it was a structural defect affecting four named personas across three distinct transfers, with amount-drift on the same transfer producing three different ledger values, and the only correct fix being the introduction of a single source-of-truth transfer-demonstration constant referenced by every roster, narrative screen, and audit screen across the file. Calling the issue a P2 polish task in the prior session's handoff had under-scoped severity by three levels. Sixteen new carry-forward entries were created and prioritised: eight P0 blockers covering the persona collision, the registry-identifier holes, the duplicate gap identifiers, the orphan carry-forwards, the screen-count off-by-one drift, the em-dash global sweep, the device-frame clipping decision, and the velocity-tax band threshold canonical-drift; eight P1 should-fix entries covering the forbidden-noun copy violations, fabric-identifier format unification, primitive-name reconciliation, missing cross-cutting primitives, terminology self-contradictions, readiness-matrix application, avatar-menu sign-out artboard, and direct-queue type-aware sample data; and four P2 and P3 entries for dead-code cleanup, future-module arithmetic and temporal drift, primitive extraction, and filename hygiene.

**Parallel Architectural Specification Track for the Command Centre Admin Coverage:** Mid-session, the operator pivoted scope away from grinding through the audit's P0 ladder and toward a separate but related concern: the audit had cataloged what was drawn, but a structural gap existed in what was never drawn — namely the operator surfaces for on-chain pool operations, three-eyes ceremonies viewed from the steward side, inter-government lending, partner financial-service-provider lifecycle, and profit settlement across the universal-basic-income and operating pools. A three-track multi-agent orchestration was launched in parallel with the audit closure: a main track wrote thirteen specification sections covering pool taxonomy, the admin role model, four canonical approval patterns, an operation catalogue, state machines, a cross-cutting compliance mixin, cross-pool flows, data aggregation, real-time channels, approval workflow with payload attestation, gap classification, and standards alignment against five enterprise-audit standards; a sibling track appended phase narratives to the work record concurrently; and a preparatory track pre-computed operation-catalogue rows, cross-pool flow diagrams, and standards-alignment tables before the main track needed them. A nine-agent three-wave super-engineer review board was convened in advance of any spec writing and produced thirty-seven findings including five P0 blockers that forced a planned highest-coverage scope to be reconciled against actual on-chain support, with sections lacking backend support reclassified as deferred-register entries rather than fabricated buildable surfaces.

**Foundational Decision Locks and Adversarial Security Findings:** Five foundational architectural decisions were locked through the review board's adversarial pass, each load-bearing for downstream specification sections. A hybrid-by-pool distribution policy was locked, mapping retail-direct rails for genesis and operations pools while binding charity disbursal through verified non-governmental tier-two distributors and loan origination through partner financial-service-provider tier-two distributors. Four canonical approval patterns were locked: single-signer for low-risk administrative operations, three-eyes for medium-risk pool operations, external-authority requiring a regulator or court cosigner for freeze and seizure operations, and threshold-signing requiring N-of-M for genesis mint, emergency pause, and super-administrator reduction. A cross-cutting compliance-mixin topology was locked, replacing a bolt-on compliance section with a deterministic five-gate preflight strip running before every decision body in the canonical order of sanctions screening, politically-exposed-person screening, travel-rule evaluation, legal-hold check, and retention-lineage annotation. The most consequential adversarial finding was that on-chain three-eyes enforcement was role-binding rather than identity-binding, allowing a single human holding two role credentials to satisfy the gate on their own transaction. Three cryptographic primitives were nominated as landing-required to close the gap: a cosigner-identity-proof binding role to identity at signing time, a mint-approval-binding tying the approval signature to the exact mint amount, and an operator-decision-binding tying the approval signature to the rendered user-interface state hash.

**Parallel Admin Console Production Remediation:** Concurrent with the audit and architecture tracks, a separate admin-console remediation workstream executed against the marketing-site administrative platform. A six-audit super-engineer review board produced one hundred fifty-five unified findings across sixty-five clusters including twenty-five P0 issues. Adversarial security review found eight new P0 vulnerabilities beyond the existing register, including a carriage-return-line-feed mail-header injection through a full-name input, an open-redirect on the click-tracking endpoint, a database role with rewrite privileges on the audit-log table, an audit endpoint with zero role-based access control, twenty-one npm vulnerabilities including one critical, and a maintenance-bypass password inlined into client JavaScript bundles. Remediation was executed across eight sequential lanes covering Redis eviction-policy correction, audit role-based access control enforcement, database permission revocation, a new health-probe endpoint with per-component breakdown, container-tag pinning, and a single-line-change container-build heap bump that resolved an out-of-memory regression appearing first at the workflow level and re-emerging inside the buildkit-isolated container build. A six-agent two-wave fix-agent fleet was dispatched against twenty-five remediation clusters in parallel, with conflict-avoidance file-ownership rules producing zero merge conflicts across forty-seven commits to a single branch. Fifty-one commits landed across seven pushes with the final production deploy carrying security boundary closures, compliance posture upgrades, operations resilience, accessibility improvements, and a Vitest test infrastructure scaffold.

**Cross-Phase Insight Consolidation and Project-Wide Patterns:** Several insights surfaced across all three tracks that reframe how prior session work should be interpreted. Persona drift was treated as cosmetic when it was structural; the fix is not renaming a row but introducing a transfer-demonstration constant referenced everywhere. The cross-cutting phase shipped every cross-cutting state but missed every cross-cutting primitive, exposing a category confusion between participant-facing states and reusable primitive components. Registry integrity is invisible shadow work that no individual auditor can detect because it manifests only when a single auditor spans every artefact at once. The em-dashes-in-supporting-documents but not in HTML files revealed exactly where the no-em-dashes discipline had internalised and where it had silently lapsed, suggesting a pre-commit hook as the durable prevention. The device-frame clipping issue is ergonomic for the project reviewer rather than structural for the participant in production, but it requires a project-wide decision between raising frame height per content-dense artboard and switching the inner pad to scrollable overflow. The three security primitives nominated as landing-required now form an explicit gate on any wireframe that draws a three-eyes ceremony, which means specification velocity will track on-chain primitive landing rather than design-team capacity.

Outcome:
The day delivered a four-hundred-thirty-five-line canonical audit report consolidating one hundred thirty-four raw findings from an eight-agent audit fleet into fifty-two canonical gaps with severity, source attribution, and a P0 ladder ready for next-session execution; a complete thirteen-section, two-thousand-four-hundred-eleven-line architectural specification for the command-centre administrative platform with a six-hundred-sixteen-line participant-side micro-specification and a one-hundred-eighty-seven-line operations registry tracking fifty-eight central-bank-grade operations across spec, wireframe, and backend status tuples; forty-six new pass-two wireframe screens spanning pool operations, pool policy, and a deferred-operations register; and fifty-one production-deployed commits closing approximately thirty P0 remediation clusters across security, compliance, operations, accessibility, and supply-chain hardening — with all of it captured in append-only narrative blocks across a single shared work-record file without merge conflict.
