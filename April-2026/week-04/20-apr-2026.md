Title: Phase 4 Desktop Admin Wireframe Build & Polish, Marketing Site Brand-Consistency Redesign, and Headless Playwright Verification

Objective:
To execute the Phase 4 wireframe build for the unified admin platform by translating a plan-locked 34-screen blueprint into a desktop-primary specification using a persistent sidebar shell, type-aware branding, and five new institution-aware primitives, then drive the file through a three-reviewer polish board to surface narrative seams and primitive-type-guard violations. The secondary objective was to close out the marketing site's content-parity work by redesigning previously shipped Gen-Z and Global-Impact sections so that visually correct content sat inside the established brand vocabulary rather than reading as flat paragraphs sandwiched between branded sections, and to verify both deliverables through automated headless Chromium runs.

Work Carried Out:

**Pivot from Mobile-Primary to Desktop-Primary Wireframe Discipline:** The Phase 4 build represented a deliberate departure from earlier phases, which had all targeted a 360x740 mobile device frame for the participant wallet surfaces. The unified admin platform is an operator console used at a desk with a full keyboard, so the canvas was flipped to a 1240x780 DesktopFrame with a persistent left sidebar carrying nine navigation items and a pending-badge row, a top bar containing a breadcrumb plus action chips plus a session countdown plus a notification bell, and a primary content pane that adapted layout per screen. Several primitives that worked at mobile density needed to breathe in the wider canvas, particularly horizontal compliance pill strips that would have stacked vertically and consumed half the viewport on a phone. The shell was also made type-aware so that the same chrome could host three institution archetypes with appropriate vocabulary: navigation labels rebranded for government context with "Members" becoming "Officials" and "Signer rules" becoming "Three-eyes rules," ensuring that primitive type-guards held across the file.

**Phase 4 Build Across Eight Sections and Thirty-Four Screens:** The shipped wireframe file totalled 2,301 lines and 144 KB, organised into eight sections covering the full administrator journey. Section A delivered the shell plus three dashboard variants spanning login with OAuth2 PKCE messaging, a multi-institution selector, a Business dashboard surfacing balance and stakeholder summary plus a four-column compliance pill strip, an NPO dashboard with a PENDING_VERIFICATION banner and document submission tracking, a Government dashboard with aggregate treasury balance and weekly genesis-mint totals, and an onboarding wizard. Sections B-biz and B-gov split stakeholder management between business permission matrices with a six-flag toggle metaphor and government official appointment with a ten-role picker plus three scope levels. Section C covered rule management with a priority-ordered list, a type-branched RuleBuilder, a simulator producing a priority-ladder output with per-row rationale, and conflict warnings at save time. Section D delivered pending-approval queues including a phase-aware government variant with PENDING_APPROVAL, PENDING_AUTHORIZATION, and RETURNED_FOR_INFO tabs. Sections E, F, and G covered compliance and credentials, audit surfaces with before-and-after diff rendering, and four error-state shells respectively.

**Five New Institution-Aware Primitives Introduced:** The build introduced five new primitives sized and tested for desktop density. The InstitutionTypePill rendered four colour-coded variants for Business in green, NPO in purple, Government in blue, and Partner in amber, available at small and large sizes for use in tables and headers respectively. The RuleBuilder composite accepted a type prop and branched its rendered form: business and NPO contexts showed a single required-approvers count with a role constraint, while government contexts showed three separate count cards for Initiator, Approver, and Authorizer with colour-keyed alignment to the GovtRolePill. The RuleSimulator rendered four sections covering a header with a pending readiness badge, an input grid for amount and recipient and category, a priority ladder listing every rule with match-or-skip status, and an output block computing the fee estimate. The CompliancePill carried four states across verified, pending, expired, and missing with optional expiry text. The AuditDiff rendered a two-column before-and-after grid with row-level highlighting that tinted the before column red and the after column green when a value had changed, leaving unchanged rows transparent. Eleven additional primitives were reused from earlier phases including ScopedContextBanner, FabricIdBadge, ReadinessBadge, the institution-context-specific OrgStatusPill and TreasuryStatusPill, GovtRolePill across ten role types, the six-flag StakeholderPermissionMatrix, the amount-threshold SignatoryRuleBand, the phase-aware ThreeEyesTally, HierarchyAccountBreadcrumb, and an ErrShell pattern adapted to desktop as ErrFrame.

**Three-Reviewer Polish Board and Narrative-Seam Catches:** A three-reviewer polish board was dispatched against the shipped file. A canonical-brand reviewer audited the file against the locked concept rules, language specification, and Prisma schemas. A UX completeness reviewer walked all thirty-four screens and cross-checked data coherence across the multi-type narrative. A Playwright verifier rendered the file headlessly. The canonical-brand reviewer caught two P0 violations: a SignatoryRuleBand intended for institute multi-signer contexts had been rendered inside a government-context approval detail screen, breaking the type-guard rule that three-eyes screens must not host institute primitives, and a reviewer-note body had used the word "User" where the canonical term "operator" or "participant" was required. The UX reviewer caught the most consequential finding, a three-screen persona contradiction where the audit log attributed an approval to one persona while the approval-detail screen showed a different persona signing as Approver for the same transfer. Neither screen was wrong in isolation; the contradiction surfaced only when a reader followed a single transaction across screens, which validated the polish-pass discipline of reading the file as a continuous narrative rather than as a collection of primitives.

**Polish-Pass Edits and Re-Verification:** Six additional fixes landed on the same polish pass to tighten the narrative. The ThreeEyesTally was extended to render TX_EXPIRED and TX_CANCELLED terminal states so that all nine of the pending-transaction status phases became visually distinct rather than only six of them. Pending-approval queue rows for the Business and NPO variants were updated to carry canonical institute fabric identifiers in the documented twenty-character grouped format rather than empty placeholder strings. The government queue rows were diversified across the three phase tabs so that the demo provided meaningful coverage of each state. Compliance readiness was changed from a wireframe-in-progress flag to a pending-backend flag to align with the locked specification. The conflict-warning primary CTA was flipped from "save anyway" to "edit rule" so the remediate path became the safer default. Several copy strings were tightened, including a clarification of "Directors or Signers" in the rule band copy. Playwright re-verification after the fixes confirmed zero console errors, zero page errors, intact title, and clean rendering of all primitives across all thirty-four artboards.

**Marketing Site Brand-Redesign Pass on Gen-Z and Global-Impact Pages:** A separate workstream addressed visual drift on two interactive analysis pages where prior content-parity work had shipped textually correct material that did not match the site's established brand vocabulary. A visual smoke confirmed the concern: the new sections rendered correct paragraphs but sat in plain white voids without eyebrow pills, without the gradient display headings, without the staggered reveal animation on first paint, and without the alternating section-background rhythm that the rest of the site used to carry the eye through long pages. An accordion primitive that had been introduced for accessibility compliance had become a visual liability by hiding informational prefaces behind a generic expand control where the rest of the site used always-visible cards and narrative prose. Six components were redesigned with their content held constant: the two About primers were converted into three-card grid sections with eyebrow pills, gradient display headings, and colour-accented top borders. The two methodology sections were unwrapped from the accordion shell so the already-branded inner cards could surface directly. The two disclaimer sections were given proper section shells with amber eyebrow pills, gradient headings, and a zinc background tone so the inner caution callout read as a considered caveat rather than a warning in a void.

**Parallel Multi-Agent Implementation and a Pattern-Source Regression:** The redesign was executed by four parallel implementation tracks dispatched from a single message: one track handled the Global-Impact trio and a stat-card hover regression, a second handled the Gen-Z trio and its parallel hover fix, and a third polished the shared accordion primitive's focus ring and border tokens without touching its semantics or keyboard handling. Ten commits landed on the development branch including one fixup commit. The fixup was occasioned by a pattern-source mismatch between the parallel agents: the first agent had used inline Feather-style SVG icon components in the Global-Impact About primer per the project's established convention documented in an existing shell component, while the second agent had followed the visual outcome and reached for a popular icon package that was not in the dependency tree. The TypeScript compiler caught the missing module immediately, and the fixup converted three icons to inline SVG components using exactly the pattern the first agent had applied. The regression confirmed a discipline lesson: parallel agents that mirror each other's patterns must be pinned to the same reference pattern source in their dispatch prompts rather than to a described visual outcome.

**Headless Playwright Verification Across Both Tracks:** Both deliverables were verified through headless Chromium runs. For the wireframe, an initial run after the build pass confirmed that the title matched, that there were zero console errors and zero page errors, that all thirty-four artboards rendered, and that all sections were visible. Two em dashes were caught in an AuditDiff fallback glyph used when a key was missing from either side of a comparison object, and the glyph was replaced with a mid-dot before re-verification confirmed a clean run. For the website redesign, end-of-phase gates were enforced through a TypeScript compilation pass, a lint pass with only pre-existing warnings, a full production build compiling all forty routes, an em-dash grep returning zero hits across the affected component directory, and a Playwright smoke suite passing six of six specs including the brand and content tripwires that had been seeded earlier in the parity work. Live visual smoke on a representative country page confirmed that the calculation footnote, the dynamic insight callout, and the assumption bullets all rendered correctly inside the redesigned shells.

**Documentation Deltas and Phase Locking:** Documentation updates accompanied both tracks. For the wireframe, an OPEN-DECISIONS group was added covering six new decisions with canonical citations, a Phase 4 gap-register summary table was appended, four new carry-forwards were queued covering a simulator endpoint, a country-business-types configuration endpoint, a hardware-key two-factor protocol, and an audit-log export, and the index file was updated to mark Phase 4 as locked at thirty-four screens. A UX-FLAWS section was added with ten Phase 4 gaps, recording seven P0 items resolved in the wireframe and three P1 items flagged for backend follow-up. For the marketing site, the redesign was committed file-by-file across the submodule with a parent-pointer bump, and CI rebuilt and deployed through the blue-green pipeline so that the live site carried both content parity and brand consistency.

Outcome:
Phase 4 of the wireframe set was locked and polished at thirty-four screens across eight sections with five new primitives and eleven reused primitives, and the marketing site's Gen-Z and Global-Impact pages were redesigned across six components into the established brand vocabulary while preserving all verbatim content, with both tracks verified through headless Chromium runs producing zero console and page errors.
