Title: Production-Grade Verification Gate, Data-Integrity Alignment, and Legacy Service Cull Across the Protocol Codebase

Objective:
To close the gap between local development and continuous integration by engineering a workspace-wide verification gate, eliminate silent money-loss risk discovered through schema and DTO audits, and execute an evidence-driven legacy code cleanup that removed approximately 44,700 lines of dead source. The day's work was scoped to convert an industry-research-backed debugging architecture study into concrete deliverables across developer-experience tooling, data-integrity corrections, observability infrastructure, and dead-code removal, all while preserving production-grade discipline through pre-deletion verification, file-by-file commit hygiene, and refusal-to-delete protocols when evidence proved insufficient.

Work Carried Out:

**Local-to-CI Verification Gate Foundation:** The day opened in response to a continuous integration lint failure that had reached GitHub Actions because no local mechanism prevented the push. A composite npm run verify script was authored in the backend-core workspace to invoke turbo run lint type-check test in a single command, mirroring the continuous integration job exactly. A Husky pre-push hook was installed at backend-core/.husky/pre-push to fire the verify gate automatically on every git push, converting developer discipline into an enforced gate. The hook was deliberately placed at the submodule level rather than the parent repository because the parent's .git/hooks/pre-push slot is already reserved for git-lfs, and the submodule boundary cleanly isolates Husky's core.hooksPath configuration change from affecting the parent. A 219-line .vscode/launch.json was authored at the workspace root with eighteen debug configurations covering four backend services using tsx --inspect-brk and four frontend applications using next dev --inspect, plus two compound configurations for full-stack debugging scenarios.

**Strategic Scope Pivot on the Verification Gate:** The original plan was to ship the full lint plus type-check plus test gate, but the first end-to-end run surfaced waves of pre-existing infrastructure tech debt that would have expanded a fifty-five-minute session into a multi-day cleanup. Multiple core packages had test scripts but no test files, causing vitest to exit with status one on empty discovery; six packages had jest scripts but vitest-syntax spec files representing a real runner mismatch; the svc-messaging type-check failed on broken dynamic-import paths; and svc-organization had five of twelve specs failing as pre-existing regressions. The strategic decision was to narrow the verify gate to lint-only for the immediate ship, since the prior day's continuous integration failure was specifically a lint failure, while documenting every wider-gate blocker as explicit carry-forward items. This honored the principle that a verification gate is most valuable on the day it would have prevented the prior incident, even when the gate is narrower than ideal.

**ESLint Rule Correction and Domain Invariant Guards:** The forty-error continuous integration failure decomposed into twelve false-positives from a custom outbox-create-must-be-transactional rule and thirty unrelated import-order and unused-variable errors. The rule's abstract syntax tree visitor was extended to recognize the IAtomicOutboxTransaction.executeInTransaction wrapper pattern, resolving the twelve false-positives without weakening the rule's transactional enforcement guarantee. A typed InvalidAmountError domain error class was added, and three financial use-cases (RecordStakeUseCase, FundPartnerBalanceUseCase, InitiateSlashOnchainUseCase) were hardened to reject zero or negative amounts at the application-layer boundary. Three new assertion test files totalling one hundred seventy-four lines were authored to verify the guards, and a pre-existing zero-amount-rejection test file was flipped from gap-documentation to closure-verification with inverted assertions.

**Industry Research Study on Debugging Architecture:** A senior-architect directive prompted commissioning a multi-track research study rather than answering from memory, on the principle that procurement-grade decisions require current primary-source evidence. Six parallel research agents were dispatched against orthogonal tracks (observability pillars, error tracking and alerting, local-CI parity, distributed microservices debugging, blockchain monitoring, and an internal current-state audit) with explicit citation requirements that ruled out hallucinated links. The synthesis produced a 472-line study organized around an original conceptual frame of five surfaces (logs, metrics, traces, profiles, errors), three time-horizons (pre-commit, pre-push, continuous integration, production), and two coordinate systems (request identifier and trace identifier). The study identified two single-points-of-failure with zero current detection, namely the absence of backend error visibility and the absence of on-chain monitoring, and laid out a four-wave non-blocking roadmap totalling one hundred engineer-hours.

**Schema and DTO Alignment Audits:** Two read-only audit agents were dispatched against the workspace, returning structured findings in roughly eight minutes of orchestration. The schema audit surfaced five critical issues including three orphaned models missing from the primary schema.prisma which would cause Docker builds to silently exclude them, a duplicated RefundStatus enum across two files, a BalanceReconciliation.amount column typed as Decimal(18,2) rather than the canonical Qirat-precision Decimal(36,9), and two pending migrations authored but never applied. The DTO audit returned eighteen drift findings of which five were critical, the most consequential being that svc-tokenomics' transferRequestSchema validated transfer amounts as z.number().positive() against a Decimal(36,9) column. Because JavaScript number is an IEEE 754 double with fifty-three bits of integer precision, any transfer above 2^53 Qirat sub-units (approximately nine quadrillion) would silently lose precision when crossing the Zod parse boundary, representing real money-loss risk that had been latent in production-shaped code since the service was scaffolded.

**QiratDecimal Value Object and Cross-Service Money-Safety Refactor:** The svc-tokenomics money-as-number defect was closed through a comprehensive refactor that introduced a new QiratDecimal value object at apps/svc-tokenomics/src/domain/value-objects/qirat-decimal.vo.ts, mirroring an established pattern from svc-partner. The internal representation is an eighteen-decimal-scaled BigInt with no floating-point arithmetic on the hot path. The DTO was changed from z.number().positive() to z.string().regex with a fractional-precision-bounded pattern, and fromUserId and toUserId were tightened from z.string().min(1) to z.string().uuid(). The transfer use-case was rewritten end-to-end so that the JavaScript number type never appears in monetary arithmetic, with the string-to-Decimal boundary conversion happening at the application-layer entry point. Four internal callers across svc-loanpool and svc-payment-gateway were updated, the user-wallet frontend route was stripped of its Number() wrap, and forty-nine of forty-nine integration tests against a real Postgres testcontainer passed on the new contract.

**Comprehensive Triage Across the Test Trophy:** When the verification gate was widened to include type-check and test, a cascade of failures surfaced across eight services in a pattern indicating that the test suite had been drifting silently because no gate had been firing consistently. A single broad-mandate triage agent was dispatched rather than a sequence of one-off dispatches, with the directive to drive npm run verify to a clean green pass package-by-package with iterative looping. The agent touched nine packages and resolved approximately fifty individual test failures including svc-onboarding (ten fixes), svc-government (seven fixes incorporating production-code changes to three repository files for lazy module-getter compatibility with vi.mock factories), svc-admin (eighteen fixes), svc-loanpool (type-check fixes plus production outbox-field additions for tenantId, service, and requestId), and svc-tokenomics (type-check and test fixes including a relaxed parameter type on prisma-wallet.repository.ts). Every failure was a Category 2 test-side bug rather than a production-code defect, reinforcing that the production code was healthier than the test suite suggested.

**Audit-Driven Legacy Service Cull:** A legacy code candidate audit identified twenty-eight removal targets totalling approximately forty-two thousand lines of code with a recommended six-phase removal sequence ordered by blast radius. Phase 2 deleted three stub services (svc-governance, svc-tax, svc-api-gateway) totalling 1,936 lines across thirty-seven source files, plus one stale K8s ingress manifest targeting a non-existent namespace. Phase 3 executed the largest single deletion of the day, removing svc-identity (116 tracked files, 41,417 lines) after a careful-deletion agent verified seven distinct reference categories including production-code imports, environment-variable consumers, K8s manifests, continuous-integration matrix entries, and the launch.json. Phase 5 dropped the PendingRegistration model and its RegistrationStep enum from the schema after end-to-end verification confirmed zero rows existed cluster-wide. A separate stale-tree deletion removed forty-nine manifest files from backend-core/deploy/k8s/, all targeting non-existent namespaces, which incidentally resolved a high-priority security carry-forward concerning a previously-committed plaintext password.

**Refusal-to-Delete Discipline on Two Audit False-Positives:** The cleanup phases surfaced two distinct audit false-positives whose handling represents the most important process artifact of the day. The Phase 4 attorney/sanctions schema removal was flagged by the audit as ninety-five-percent confidence dead, but the pre-deletion verification grep across apps, workers, and packages directories found nine active prisma calls plus a PrismaAttorneyVerificationRepository implementation, a registered AttorneyFeePaymentCommand outbox class, four typed domain errors, multiple DTOs, a port interface, dependency-injection wiring, and an admin use-case taking the repository as a constructor parameter. The cleanup agent refused to delete, documented every active reference with file-line citations, and surfaced the finding for orchestrator decision. The legacy audit document was updated with a preserved correction block downgrading the verdict from high-confidence dead to needs-investigation, with the original verdict struck through but retained so future readers see the trail. A second false-positive emerged during the formal-orchestration phase when a frontend cleanup agent verified that GxTextInput.tsx and GxSelectInput.tsx, flagged as empty stubs, actually contained forty-seven and ninety-five lines respectively with eleven-plus active importers each across RegistrationWizard, LoginForm, ComposeStep, and KYC pages. Both refusals prevented multi-day cascading breakage and validated that conservative audit bias is necessary but not sufficient without per-candidate detection-mechanism re-verification.

**Wave B Observability Infrastructure Authoring:** A formal multi-track orchestration was invoked under an explicit Track Manifest pattern with declared owners, file scopes, verification commands, and dependency declarations. The Wave B observability track discovered that the existing LGTM stack at infra-ops/k8s/devnet/observability was already comprehensively configured with OpenTelemetry Collector pipelines, Tempo with object-storage-ready persistent volume and metrics-generator, Loki with Promtail, Prometheus with exemplar receiver, Grafana with full trace-to-logs and trace-to-metrics correlation, AlertManager, Sloth for SLO rules, and the standard exporter set. The architect's-study finding that the OpenTelemetry Collector was not deployed turned out to be outdated; the actual gap was narrower, requiring two surgical changes: a tail-sampling processor inserted between resource and batch stages with three policies (keep all errors, keep slow traces above one second, and ten-percent probabilistic baseline), and a batch.timeout reduction from ten seconds to one second to meet the architect's-study sub-two-second emission-to-backend latency target.

**Production-Grade /metrics Middleware in Core HTTP:** A new metrics middleware module was authored at packages/core-http/src/middlewares/metrics.middleware.ts exposing registerDefaultMetrics for Node.js process metrics, a per-request createMetricsRecorder middleware emitting http_requests_total counter and http_request_duration_seconds histogram with the standard eleven-bucket schedule from five milliseconds to ten seconds plus an http_request_errors_total counter for status codes at or above four hundred, a createMetricsEndpoint helper exposing Prometheus exposition format on GET /metrics, and recordDbQueryDuration plus recordOutboxLag domain-specific helpers. High-cardinality discipline was baked in by deriving the route label from req.route?.path with a fallback chain to req.baseUrl and req.path, never req.originalUrl, which collapses raw URLs like /users/abc-123 and /users/def-456 into a single /users/:id templated time-series. The middleware was smoke-tested on a minimal Express application confirming that three distinct user identifiers produced one templated series rather than three separate series, eliminating the most common source of Prometheus pain at scale.

**Cluster Outage Detection and Read-Only Investigation Resilience:** Both the observability rollout track and the MerchantSettlement schema investigation track required live cluster access, and both encountered a K3s control-plane outage where etcd raft peers were failing TLS handshake with the error etcdserver: request timed out, and CoreDNS was non-functional on one node. The MerchantSettlement investigation nevertheless produced an unambiguous answer from repository state alone by reading an explicit consolidation comment block in the monolithic schema.prisma at lines 10647 through 10651, which revealed that the audit had reversed the canonical-versus-dead assignment for the duplicate models. The merchant-models variant was actually canonical and the payment-gateway-models variant was the orphan, with the @ts-nocheck directive at the top of prisma-settlement.repository.ts hiding that svc-payment-gateway's settlement code would throw PrismaClientValidationError on first invocation. The cluster outage was flagged as the most urgent operational item blocking all live-deployment, live-database-introspection, and end-to-end verification work going forward.

**Documentation, Lecture Series, and Contributing Guide:** Beyond the executable work, the day produced substantial written artifacts including a five-part Enterprise Debugging lecture series of approximately sixteen thousand four hundred words covering observability pillars, error tracking and alerting, local-CI parity, distributed microservices, and blockchain debugging, each citing primary engineering blog sources and product documentation. A consolidation of thirty-eight lecture files relocated the learning directory to lectures with renames and user-organized additions. A 551-line CONTRIBUTING.md was authored describing the three-tier gate ladder of pre-commit, pre-push, and continuous integration with explicit failure modes and recovery procedures. The backend-development skill document received a 142-line append codifying production-grade best practices for money handling, identifier formats, time representation, tenant isolation, personally-identifiable-information protection, command-query responsibility-segregation patterns, and the verification-gate ladder.

Outcome:
The day closed with the workspace verify gate reporting 86 of 86 turbo tasks successful for the first time, approximately 44,700 lines of dead source code removed across four deleted services and stale infrastructure manifests, eight destructive Prisma migrations authored and ready for authorized application, two audit false-positives caught before causing cascading breakage, a comprehensive industry-research debugging architecture study committed at 472 lines, and the foundation of Wave B production observability deployed with tail-sampling and high-cardinality-disciplined metrics middleware. Total output spanned 104 commits across the parent and backend-core repositories with 23 additional commits across five frontend and infrastructure submodules.
