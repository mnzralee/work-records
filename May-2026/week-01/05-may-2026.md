Title: Schema Audit and Eight-Migration Apply, Twelve-Iteration CI Cascade Recovery, Multi-Agent Bug Hunt, and End-to-End Architecture Phase for the Naming Service Registrar

Objective:
To clear three days of accumulated continuous-integration blockers and a degraded Kubernetes control plane by sequencing cheap codebase unblockers ahead of expensive infrastructure recovery, audit and apply eight pending Prisma migrations through five parallel correctness tracks against a live database, run a five-track multi-agent bug hunt that surfaces ninety latent schema-versus-code defects, drive the continuous integration pipeline to green across a twelve-iteration cascade of compounding root causes, and in parallel execute the complete architecture phase for the .gx naming service registrar through five sub-phases plus a nine-agent adversarial review board so that the resulting architecture is either lock-ready or has a precise resolution backlog written down.

Work Carried Out:

**Cheap Unblocker Sequencing Ahead of Cluster Recovery:** The day opened with a deliberate triage decision that shaped every subsequent track: separate the work into code-only items that are safe regardless of cluster state, and infrastructure items that are gated on Kubernetes recovery. The lock-file drift that had silently kept continuous integration red for three days was traced to two user-interface workspaces declaring `socket.io-client` at `>=4.7.0` as a peer dependency, which npm 10 auto-resolves to 4.8.3 without regenerating the lock file; the continuous integration runner uses `npm ci`, which strictly validates the lock and refuses to install. A single `npm install` in the backend-core root produced ninety-six insertions and thirty-six deletions in the lock file, exactly proportional to absorbing the four missing transitive entries `socket.io-client@4.8.3`, `engine.io-client@6.6.4`, `ws@8.18.3`, and `xmlhttprequest-ssl@2.1.2`. A clean ort merge then pulled six pending commits from origin (multi-virtual-private-server mainnet planning, three-domain DNS configuration, sanction-proof architecture revision) without a single conflict against two locally ahead commits.

**Root-Cause Resolution of a Common-Js Cycle in the Authentication Service:** The authentication service had been carrying a temporal-dead-zone failure pattern that crashed at module load with the classic "Cannot access 'DomainError' before initialization" signature. Tracing the import graph revealed that `domain/errors/index.ts` both declared `DomainError` and re-exported `PartnerContextUnavailableError` from `partner-context.error.ts`, while that sibling file imported `DomainError` from the same barrel; under the TypeScript-to-Common-Js interop, when the sibling loaded first, the require of the barrel returned a partially-initialised module where `DomainError` was still uninitialised, and the `class ... extends DomainError` declaration threw immediately. The fix extracted `DomainError` into a leaf module `base.error.ts` with zero internal dependencies, then redirected both the barrel and the sibling to import from there; the barrel's public application programming interface was preserved so every consumer continued importing `DomainError` from `domain/errors`. A runtime smoke test instantiated `PartnerContextUnavailableError` and confirmed `instanceof DomainError === true`, and the suite ran nineteen test files at one hundred sixty-nine green.

**Conservative-Bias Dead-Code Audit and Phase Two Deletion:** A previous dead-code audit had estimated one hundred eighty-six candidate files; Phase One had safely deleted nine of those at one thousand three hundred six lines. Phase Two re-generated the residual list with explicit per-candidate verification mandatory before any deletion: word-count plus workspace-wide grep, with self-exclusion patterns to defeat the false-positives that had nearly slipped through Phase One. An exploratory agent was dispatched under strict constraints (high-confidence only, more than fifty lines of code, zero importers, not in filesystem-routed application directories) and returned five candidates from one hundred seventy-seven; independent re-verification including a broader-pattern grep caught two `SidebarContext` references that turned out to be local React context declarations sharing the identifier rather than imports of the candidate. All five proved truly orphan and were deleted in file-by-file commits with descriptive bodies recording the verification evidence: three hundred ninety-four lines across `useNotificationMutations.ts`, `use-treasury.ts`, `ipfs-fallback.ts`, `use-data-table-instance.ts`, and `lib/contexts/SidebarContext.tsx`. A pre-existing TypeScript error surfaced during cleanup where the sanctions admin page referenced `screening.userDob` against a type field declared as `userDateOfBirth`, and a one-character fix was committed separately so the dead-code commits remained clean.

**Five-Track Parallel Audit of Eight Pending Database Migrations:** A `kubectl get pods` against the backend namespace returned an empty list, which clarified that the workload manifests had been part of whatever Kubernetes etcd state had rolled back during the prior day's recovery, while the data plane survived because it held persistent volume claims bound to local-path volumes that the control-plane outage could not touch. The recovery path required a known-good database state before any new pods could be brought up. Five parallel correctness audits were therefore commissioned: migration replay correctness against a throwaway database, schema drift across three sources of truth, per-migration safety review of eight pending files queued under `--create-only`, foreign-key and constraint integrity across every Prisma `@relation`, and a live inspection of the production Postgres primary via port-forward to provide ground truth. The audits returned in staggered fashion and triangulated to a single conclusion: the migration history was fundamentally incomplete because months of bringing schemas up through `prisma db push` had materialised tables directly from `schema.prisma` without authoring migration files, leaving one hundred twenty-seven tables and one hundred twenty-nine enums declared in the schema with no migration creating them. The `CommandType` enum carried seventy-six values in the schema and only thirteen in the migration trail, which would crash every outbox submission for partner-bridge, validator, loan, send, and government-treasury commands on any fresh production deploy.

**Idempotency Hardening and Clean Apply of Eight Migrations:** The migration-replay audit reported sixteen confirmed bugs across fourteen files, including three Class-A bridge-collision bugs where `ALTER TABLE ADD COLUMN` statements collided with columns already created by the chaincode bridge with no `IF NOT EXISTS` guard, plus a clever finding where the partner audit-log append-only migration used `digest(v_input, 'sha256')` from the `pgcrypto` extension without any migration installing it. The three Class-A guards were applied, the `CREATE EXTENSION IF NOT EXISTS pgcrypto` statement was added to the audit-log migration, and a placeholder migration file was created for the historical manual baselining directory that Prisma had begun refusing to load with P3015. With the prerequisites resolved, `prisma migrate deploy` applied all eight pending migrations in a single transaction-per-migration sequence in roughly twenty seconds: the `PendingRegistration` table was dropped, the `PartnerApplicationDocument` table and its supporting enum and indexes were created, three nullable biometric fields were added to `UserProfile` with range checks, the velocity-tax universal-basic-income column was retyped from `BIGINT` to `DECIMAL(36, 9)`, and decimal precision was pinned on `PartnerStakingConfig`. Verification confirmed eight new rows in `_prisma_migrations`, twenty-three user profiles preserved, twenty-six transactions preserved, forty-nine know-your-customer application documents preserved, and fifty-three outbox commands preserved.

**Comprehensive Schema Bridge Authored as an Idempotent Baseline:** With the eight migrations applied, the next step was a single corrective migration that could create every missing table and enum the migration history had skipped. `prisma migrate diff --from-empty --to-schema-datamodel` produced nine thousand four hundred ten lines of `CREATE` data-definition language, which a Python transformer post-processed to wrap each statement in idempotent guards: `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS`, `CREATE INDEX IF NOT EXISTS`, `CREATE UNIQUE INDEX IF NOT EXISTS`, and `DO $$ BEGIN ... EXCEPTION WHEN duplicate_object THEN NULL` blocks for the `CREATE TYPE` and `ALTER TABLE ADD CONSTRAINT` statements that Postgres does not accept `IF NOT EXISTS` on natively. The final file at ten thousand four hundred seventeen lines, four hundred eighty-four exception wrappers, and one thousand eighty `IF NOT EXISTS` guards was named to sort ahead of the dependent partner module chain. Applied to the development database, it created fifty-two new tables, thirty-nine new enums, four hundred eighty-seven new check constraints, and six hundred sixty-seven new columns, bringing the table count from two hundred sixteen to two hundred sixty-eight and the enum count from one hundred sixty-one to two hundred in perfect alignment with the schema's declared two hundred sixty-seven models and two hundred enums.

**Continuous Integration Cascade Resolution Across Twelve Iterations:** The schema work surfaced a deeper architectural choice about how the test pipeline materialises its database. The comprehensive bridge could not replay cleanly on a fresh database because the `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS` pattern guards against the table existing but silently skips the entire table body, so subsequent `CREATE INDEX` statements that depend on new columns failed against a partially-initialised existing table. The pragmatic decision switched the test job from `prisma migrate deploy` to `prisma db push --accept-data-loss --skip-generate`, sidestepping the bridge gap. Twelve subsequent continuous integration iterations then exposed compounding root causes: the turbo run separator does not forward flags to inner binaries, so the `--coverage` argument was being received by jest as a positional and rejected; the Prisma client mirror step used `cp -r` into an existing directory, producing a nested doubled path that the type-checker could not navigate; the underlying disease was a custom generator output path of `../../packages/core-db/node_modules/.prisma/client` in the schema, which violated the ecosystem assumption that the client lives next to the placeholder package at root and forced the copy-dance workaround across twenty service Dockerfiles; a deprecated module-load Zod schema in the shared configuration package validated `JWT_SECRET` at import time and crashed any test file that transitively imported anything from it; the `@gx/test-utils` workspace package declared `main: dist/index.js` without anything having built the dist directory before tests ran. Each iteration's fix surfaced the next deeper choice nobody had questioned, until the prettier check ran on a fresh state and reported two thousand thirty-seven files of style drift plus two hard errors on a four-point-one gigabyte performance dump checked into the repository as JavaScript Object Notation.

**Multi-Agent Bug Hunt Across Five Orthogonal Tracks:** With the surface continuous integration errors stabilising, five super-engineer agents were dispatched in parallel under non-overlapping briefs so that the union of findings would cover more ground than any single deep-dive: bridge migration column-gap analysis, lint job piece verification, continuous integration requirements audit, Prisma enum drift hunt, schema-versus-code field-name drift hunt, and nullability drift hunt. The combined output was approximately ninety distinct defects across twenty-five source files. The schema-versus-code track returned twenty-eight critical drifts including four projector handlers that query primary-key columns under names the schema does not have (`BudgetCycle.budgetCycleId` and `TransitionEvent.transitionId` against schemas where the primary key is simply `id`), a confirmed `MerchantSettlement` schema fork between the payment-gateway repository and the canonical model, and `Dispute` domain logic referencing `version`, `reopenCount`, and `reopenedAt` fields that simply do not exist in the schema. The enum-drift track returned forty-two findings of which six were critical because they were writing values the schema rejects, including `ContractStatus 'TERMINATED'` in the loan-pool service and `TreasuryStatus 'TREASURY_FROZEN'` across four government-service sites where the canonical value is `TREASURY_INACTIVE`. The nullability-drift track surfaced a Wallet schema dual-source divergence between the monolithic and multi-file schemas, plus two handlers each issuing dueling `updateMany` queries against mutually-exclusive identifier formats so that one query silently matches zero rows on every event.

**Wave-Driven Mechanical Sweeps Across Schema-Versus-Code Drifts:** With the bug ledger documented, the resolution work was sequenced as a wave model: each wave was internally homogeneous (mechanical renames, enum migrations, Dockerfile cleanup, version alignment) so that any mid-wave breakage was easy to triage, and waves were gated on continuous integration green before the next dispatched. Wave A renamed five mechanical schema-versus-code drifts including the `TreasuryStatus` rename across four government-service files, a `Conversation.name` to `groupName` rename in the messaging service, a `MerchantRefundApproval.createdAt` to `votedAt` rename, and a `BatchPaymentItem.processedAt` to `completedAt` rename, plus a time-bombed three-eyes integration specification that had hardcoded `expiresAt='2026-05-05T10:00:00Z'` and was fixed with `vi.useFakeTimers` paired with `vi.useRealTimers` in the test lifecycle hooks. Wave B migrated five enum types from hand-rolled local definitions to canonical Prisma-generated imports, including a `NotificationType` port in the trust service that carried only three values where the canonical enum had twenty-three, silently making twenty notification kinds impossible to emit; the recipe locked in as `import { Foo } from '@prisma/client'` with value imports for runtime comparisons and `import type` only for signatures. A Dockerfile cleanup removed twenty-five orphan `COPY` directives across eight Dockerfiles for stub services purged in January, and a cross-workspace dependency audit aligned `@types/node` and `prom-client` across twenty-eight workspace package files to the Node twenty long-term support line that matches the production runtime and the runner.

**Type-System Escape-Hatch Sweep Across Two Services:** The carry-forward audit had reported sixty-two `@ts-nocheck` directives in the loan-pool service and eighty-four in the payment-gateway service. Discovery passes revealed the actual counts of seven and eighty-three respectively. The loan-pool sweep ran first: five of the seven directives were stripped cleanly, surfacing two real `Prisma.InputJsonValue` cast requirements on Json columns and three pure dead-weight directives where a stale fix-me comment had outlived its premise (the fix-me had blamed multi-file Prisma generation, but the canonical schema had been fixed in a previous session and the comment had never been cleaned up); one directive was correctly retained with a precise updated fix-me pointing at a real domain-versus-schema enum drift on `ContractStatus 'TERMINATED'` that needed a specification decision. The payment-gateway sweep then ran across seventy-seven of the eighty-three directives in eighteen file-by-file commits organised by architectural layer (entities, ports, use cases, infrastructure services, validation schemas, middleware, controllers, repositories) and surfaced eight latent production bugs in-place: three number-versus-string boundary contract violations at the tokenomics client where domain code was passing `number` against a Qirat-precision `string` signature, a `PaymentNotFoundError` constructor that ignored its `paymentId` parameter and rendered a non-actionable bare message, three TypeScript narrowing errors on parsed `unknown` response bodies in the tokenomics client error path, and two module-resolution drifts where top-level dynamic imports violated `node16` resolution. **Sub-Phase C Reference Architecture Across Five Views:** With Sub-phase B locked, a master integrating view was drafted at six hundred sixty lines following the C-four model, decomposing the registrar into eight bounded contexts (registry, auction, billing, search, audit, admin, identity-bridge, chain-projector) under the Newman two-hop rule, walking through four critical data flows (registration, resolution, English auction with anti-sniping, Vickrey sealed-bid with commit-reveal mechanics), and cataloguing nine cross-cutting patterns. Four subsidiary views (data, integration, security, operations) were then dispatched as parallel agents under a shared brief and landed within an hour of each other at a cumulative three thousand twenty-three lines, with cross-document consistency verified by tracing the identity-bridge, the Discovery contract, and the audit log across all five views.

**Super Engineer Review Board Across Nine Agents in Three Waves:** With the architecture corpus complete at approximately one hundred forty-five thousand words across one hundred seventy-four files, a nine-agent review board was dispatched in three waves: a research-validation wave covering citation accuracy, source completeness, and quality-attribute derivation; an architectural-critique wave covering cross-document consistency, hidden coupling, and alternatives-audit; and an adversarial-security wave covering threat-model gaps, regulatory traps, and operational gaps. The cumulative output was approximately four thousand seven hundred forty-three lines across nine review documents, surfacing approximately two hundred fifty findings of which sixteen require architectural revision before lock. The most consequential findings appeared in multiple waves from independent lenses, which raised confidence in their materiality: the ninety-nine-point-nine-nine-nine-percent auction availability service-level objective is mathematically incompatible with a one-minute auction recovery-time objective and a five-to-fifteen-minute Postgres failover, requiring either a relaxation of the service-level objective or a true active-active multi-region architecture for the auction service from year one; the regulatory-traps wave surfaced a direct internal contradiction in the source specifications between fiat-only collection and ground-experience-denominated auction deposits that, if left unresolved, would pull the registrar entity into the Monetary Authority of Singapore Payment Services Act Digital-Payment-Token licence regime and destroy the Singapore independent-tech-company structure; an Office-of-Foreign-Assets-Control strict-liability sanctions race was identified in the cache time-to-live design where a twenty-four-hour clear cache plus asynchronous provider verification creates a one-to-four-hour window during which a sanctioned registrant can pass screening.
