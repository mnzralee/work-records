Title: Multi-Arc Execution Day: Recurring Cast Completion, Three-Minute Generative Film Assembly, Internal Beta Reconciliation, Production Incident Restoration, and a Hollow-Trophy Test Tier Build-Out

Objective:
To execute and verify four concurrent engineering arcs in a single working day spanning a generative film production pipeline, an Internal Beta launch reconciliation against a migrated cluster substrate, a production incident restoration on a public marketing site, and a deep test-tier remediation following an authentication regression. The work covered finishing five remaining recurring characters for a clay-style video series via a controlled hybrid generation method, authoring a deterministic shot generation specification and producing a complete first three-minute assembly, reconciling a stale launch dispatch against a migrated Kubernetes substrate through a Super Engineer Review Board pass, restoring a downed contact form pipeline and hardening the deploy path against environment and network misconfiguration, and building the missing integration test tier that exposed two latent production defects in the admin console pipeline.

Work Carried Out:

**Recurring Cast Completion Through the Path-B Hybrid Generation Pattern:** The day opened on the generative film arc with five remaining recurring characters to complete to production grade alongside the three already locked from the prior session. Each character was generated through the Path-B hybrid pattern, which feeds a clay-style example as the first reference and a previously locked character as the second style calibration reference, with explicit prompt framing instructing the model that the new character is style-derived but identity-distinct from both inputs. Nadia required a regeneration after a prior session committed an unapproved candidate that drifted into a Pixar-smooth glossy render rather than the matte plasticine surface treatment of the locked cast. The drifted candidate was archived under refs/archive-pixar-3d-style-drift/ per the no-deletion provenance rule, and the regenerated anchor came back matching the locked cast's matte surface, the Aardman-moderate eye treatment, and the brand-marker placement encoded by the character creation discipline. Sam, Lina, Jordan, and Kai followed in sequence with similar discipline, each receiving an anchor plus five canonical poses plus a VISUAL-DNA specification, and each committed file-by-file with provenance archives where regenerations were required.

**Prop-Composition Verification Discipline and the Pose-05 Catch:** The Sam character build surfaced a verification discipline gap that became a permanent rule for downstream work. Pose five was presented as clean, but the user identified that the model had invented a disembodied grey pole holding the phone the character was meant to be checking, because the prompt named a scooter handlebar in a framing that excluded the scooter. Two iterations were required to resolve, the first replacing the pole with a hand-held phone but rendering a scales-of-justice icon on the screen due to the word balance being ambiguous to the image model, and the second specifying an explicit numeric balance with green confirmation tick and explicitly banning scales imagery. Both rejected attempts were archived to provenance, and the lesson was encoded as two prompt-craft rules: every prop must have its full context named or the model will hallucinate a stand-in, and no word with a literal visual double-meaning may appear on a screen or sign. The remaining cast builds applied these rules forward and produced clean prop-heavy poses on the first try.

**Script Refinement Cascade and Voice-Over Re-Recording Discipline:** A separate review of the problem-01 script produced two surgical content improvements grounded in the source-of-truth document: a missing rebuttal to a predictable inflation objection was added to the answer beat, and the strongest shareable point from the parent essay regarding capital allocation injustice was integrated into the personal-cost beat. The change ran a full cascade through downstream artifacts because the six existing voice-overs were recorded against the prior wording. Only the two voice-overs whose beats changed were re-recorded against the locked Rachel voice profile, with the original takes archived to scenes/archive-script-v1-vo/ for provenance, while the other five remained untouched. The PRODUCTION-PLAN screenplay treatment, the beat-structure table durations, and every beat timecode across both the screenplay and the shot list were updated to reflect the new runtime of three minutes five seconds. Two wardrobe inconsistencies between the screenplay and the locked character references were corrected in the same pass, eliminating drift between the binding documents and the visual canon.

**Shot Generation Specification and Pre-Production Bible:** With the recurring cast complete and the hero talking-head tool initially locked to a low-cost provider through an A/B test, an honest pre-production completeness audit determined that the directorial layer of planning was complete but the generation-ready layer was not. The shot list carried fifty-one NEED markers against twenty-four HAVE markers, with approximately ten missing source stills, several pose variants outstanding, and no deterministic per-shot generation specification. A Shot Generation Specification was authored that pins each of the forty-one shots to an executable instruction comprising the exact source asset path, the exact tool, the camera or motion prompt, the lighting note inherited from a per-beat cinema-grade environment description, the voice-over span, and the duration. The specification encodes a money-efficient structural decision shot-by-shot: hero talking-head generation is restricted to the approximately fifty-four on-camera seconds rather than the full beat audio length, paid image-to-video motion is reserved for the ten shots where physical motion is essential to the storytelling, and every static insert and quick montage cut receives a free editor-side Ken Burns push.

**Standing No-Music Content Constraint and Sound Design Rewrite:** The user established a binding standing constraint that no music may appear in any series media, rooted in personal moral principles, which superseded the music cues previously prescribed in the PRODUCTION-PLAN sound-design section and the series workflow document. The constraint was recorded to project memory to prevent any future session from reintroducing music. The sound design was rewritten across both documents to deliver the previously music-carried emotional arc through three means only: the voice-over performance, intentional physical-set foley including chalk taps, counter hum, lathe whir, and footsteps, and deliberate silence. The cool-to-warm temperature arc that a scoring approach would normally lift through strings is now told entirely through the lighting temperature shift and the lead character's vocal register, which fits the intimate clay-set aesthetic better than score would have.

**Super Engineer Review Board on the Generation Specification:** A pre-execution review board was run on the Shot Generation Specification before any generation budget was committed. The board executed as two parallel lenses, one set of deterministic ground-truth checks confirming asset existence and timing alignment, and one independent reviewer agent red-teaming the specification against the script, the production plan, and the cast file. The deterministic pass confirmed the sixteen referenced source assets existed on disk, all seven voice-overs were present, and the seven beat timings matched between the specification and the plan. The independent reviewer caught two material defects the author was blind to: every character-reference path was written as repository-relative-to-spec-folder rather than repository-relative, which would have broken the hero and cutaway batches at generation time after the still budget was already spent, and an arithmetic miss had labeled the paid image-to-video work as ten clips at one cost when the per-beat tables actually contained thirteen clips at a higher true cost. Both were corrected, two cross-document drifts including a residual music cue in the script and stale voice-actor references in the plan were scrubbed, and the verdict moved from go-with-fixes to go.

**Three-Minute Film Assembly via Script and the Eighty-Seven-Minute Bug:** With the specification locked, generation executed across stills, hero talking-head clips, and image-to-video cutaways in gated batches, with each batch verified element-by-element rather than fired blind. The hero talking-head tool was reversed from the originally-locked low-cost provider to a higher-quality alternative after a direct head-to-head comparison showed the latter rendered eye-blinks and visible teeth where the former sat waxy-still around the eyes and mouth, with the cost basis updated across the specification accordingly. With all assets generated, ffmpeg was installed on the host and an assembler script was authored that fits each beat's visuals to that beat's measured voice-over length, holds hero clips at natural duration to preserve lip-sync alignment, applies Ken Burns to static inserts, freezes the final character's last frame for the silent close, and lays the seven beat voice-overs as one continuous master track. The first render reported a video duration of five thousand two hundred and fifty seconds in an eight hundred and sixty-seven megabyte file because the zoompan filter combined with an input-side duration flag inflated Ken Burns stills to roughly frames-squared output. The fix dropped the input-side duration flag and hard-capped each zoompan output with a frame count cap, and the re-run produced a one hundred and ninety-four second cut in a fifty megabyte file with every beat's video matching its voice-over to the frame.

**Stale Dispatch Reconciliation and the Brownfield Disk-Reality Audit:** A parallel arc opened on the Internal Beta launch preparation, where the day was framed by a confident dispatch brief that turned out to be substantially stale against the disk state. The brief asserted a date two days prior to the actual system date, named a yesterday journal that did not exist, and described a substrate that had been migrated from one virtual private server to a new host two days earlier through a validator transplant procedure. A brownfield disk-reality audit was run before any execution agent was dispatched, walking through git state, the cluster pod inventory, the Kyverno policy set, the NetworkPolicy count, the Besu chain state, and every BLOCKING and CRITICAL row in the launch carry-forward matrix. The audit found twenty-two backend pods running on the new substrate, three more than the planned eighteen because three additional services had joined, full data and observability planes operational, and three defenses that did not exist on the prior substrate. The carry-forward registry was reconciled, an Architecture Decision Record was authored for the substrate change with its claims validated against the live cluster, and the launch-gating set dropped from six BLOCKING plus three CRITICAL to two unconditional BLOCKING plus two unconditional CRITICAL items.

**Standalone Output Configuration Restored After Migration Loss:** A latent build break was identified in the partner-admin-center frontend whose standalone output configuration had been lost during the substrate migration, leaving the Dockerfile contract referencing a build artifact path that no longer existed. The next Docker build of the affected frontend would have failed at the COPY instruction. The standalone output flag was reapplied to the next.config.mjs, dependencies were installed on the fresh checkout, and a real build was run that produced the seven thousand and fourteen byte standalone server.js artifact, providing definitive proof that the Dockerfile contract was satisfied. The fix was committed deliberately without bumping the parent submodule pointer to avoid colliding with a parallel arc actively writing the same parent repository.

**Credential Sourcing Migration and the Approved-Plan Surfacing Discipline:** The credential-sourcing remediation was executed against the rewritten plan with an implementation sub-agent dispatched to migrate twenty-two sites across thirteen files to the env-with-throwIfMissing idiom, with an eslint regression guard added covering both the nullish-coalescing and logical-or operators plus the specific OTP and PIN constants. The user chose the original plan path, the migration was executed, and the work was committed in two logical units by explicit path: the spec and fixture files as one coherent credential-sourcing change, and the eslint regression guard as a separate concern. The discipline encoded is that an approved plan does not license silence on material facts discovered during execution.

**Five-Hundred-Three Diagnosis on the Marketing Site Contact Form:** A third arc opened with a production incident on the public marketing site where every form submission failed with a five hundred and three response. The contact route's own source code was the first half of the diagnosis: the route runs a Redis-backed rate-limit check as its very first step, the check is deliberately fail-closed and returns a 503 with a specific rate-limiter-unavailable code when the Redis client throws, and the route itself can only emit two hundred, four hundred, five hundred, or five hundred and two responses, never 503, so the failure had to originate at the rate-limit gate. The live health endpoint confirmed the failure mode with a specific value distinguishing env var absent from service unreachable, which immediately reframed the investigation from a downed Redis hunt to an env-missing-on-container conclusion. The container had been running in this broken state for approximately six point nine days, with the public homepage health probe passing the entire time because the homepage renders without a data plane.

**Two Compounding Faults and the Blue-Green Restoration:** SSH access to the host revealed two compounding faults: the application container had loaded a stale env file lacking the data-plane variables, and the container sat on the default Docker bridge network rather than the user-defined network where the data-plane services resolved their container hostnames. The root cause behind the cause was that the continuous-integration deploy path, which is the only path that wires the network correctly, had been disabled two weeks earlier for billing, and the fallback manual deploy script pointed at the stale env file and omitted the network flag. A parallel green container was started on an unused port with the correct env file and network, health-checked in isolation while the live blue container kept serving, and traffic was flipped via the validated nginx upstream wrapper script. The public form was verified with a honeypot-filled POST returning two hundred and a validation POST returning four hundred, proving the request reached past the rate limiter.

**Three Durable Deploy-Path Hardenings and a Pre-Commit Validation:** Three file-by-file commits were authored against the the-website submodule. The first pinned the container HOSTNAME to all-interfaces in the Dockerfile because Docker injects the container ID as HOSTNAME, which caused Next standalone to bind only the eth0 IP and the in-container loopback health probe to be refused, leaving every container reporting unhealthy while serving fine. The second made the primary deploy script join the data-plane network with a preflight that fails loud if the network is gone, gate on the dependency-aware health endpoint rather than the homepage, and apply the read-only filesystem hardening with a writable tmpfs for the Next.js image-optimization cache. The third repointed the manual deploy script from the stale env file to the canonical runtime env file, added the network and cache tmpfs, and switched all five health probes from the homepage to the dependency-aware endpoint. The hardened run command was validated on a throwaway container with the new flags before commit, confirming it booted healthy, served the health endpoint with two hundred, answered the in-container loopback probe, and returned two hundred on a real image-optimization request.

**Cookie-Scope Regression Diagnosis and a Test That Encoded the Bug:** Restoring the marketing site's data plane woke up the admin console, which had been dark for six days, and the moment it woke it threw 401 on every authenticated API call. The first hypothesis of session expiry was disproven by a precise diagnostic shape: the user was on a server-rendered admin page when the API failed, meaning the middleware-protected page request had received the cookie but the client XHR to the API had not. That pointed at cookie scope, and the session module's defaultCookieOptions was setting the path narrowly to the admin segment. By RFC 6265 path-matching, a cookie scoped to one segment is not sent to a sibling segment, so the UI route tree and the API route tree shared no prefix the cookie could cover. Git archaeology confirmed the bug had been fixed once and un-fixed weeks later with a security rationale that did not survive the topology, because the HttpOnly, SameSite-Strict, and Secure flags already close the leakage concern. Two lines in session.ts changed the cookie path and the legacy-clear path, and the session unit test that had been actively asserting the broken value was flipped to assert the requirement with an inline comment naming both regressing commits to prevent a third flip.

**Local QA Harness on the New Substrate and the Hollow-Trophy Finding:** The cookie fix could not be proven end-to-end on production because that would require minting credentials on a live system, so the user authorized building a local QA harness on the new substrate. A local Postgres and Redis pair were brought up under sudo Docker, the Prisma migrate-deploy command ran the initial migration, and the application was started in dev mode against a clean test env file with the developer's real env file backed up. The assessment that reframed the effort was an honest read of the existing three hundred and seventy-three vitest tests: every one mocked the database and Redis, the integration tier was scoped in the env example file but never built, and the authed admin end-to-end was explicitly deferred. The trophy was hollow, with a fat green unit base, a thin public-surface end-to-end top, and nothing real between, which was the literal reason the cookie-path bug had shipped twice. The Playwright baseline confirmed the shape at fifty passed and twenty-one failed, with every failure in the public-forms a11y and UX layer.

**Four-Wave Parallel Agent Build-Out of the Missing Test Tier:** Four waves of disjoint-file parallel sub-agents built out the missing layers, with the orchestrator authoring prompt-writer-grade briefs covering scope, exact acceptance commands, hard constraints, and output contracts, and serializing all commits through the orchestrator. Wave one ran two agents on provably disjoint scopes covering the public-form a11y fixes and the integration tier foundation. Wave two browser-proved the cookie fix in a real Chromium context holding only the session cookie scoped to root, providing the layer of verification that the unit test structurally could not. Wave three fanned three agents across the remaining admin route groups, and the first real run against the full admin surface caught one root cause across fourteen failures: an EmailTemplate model and an email-campaign-recipients locked-at column were declared in schema.prisma but no migration had ever created them, meaning every database stopped at the initial migration was missing the templates table entirely and the admin templates feature had been quietly broken in production throwing table-does-not-exist on any use. The missing migration was generated, applied, and the suite went to one hundred and twenty-one passed. Wave four covered authed-admin axe accessibility and the edge route groups, with three test-isolation lessons surfacing during verification: a Redis-backed rate-limit-reset glob missing an in-memory limiter map and a second admin-scoped rate-limit key namespace, and a Playwright worker race against the shared test database that required forcing a single worker locally.

**Interactive UX Audit and the Production Cutover:** After a green test trophy was reported, the user asked the sharp question that the trophy had not answered: had the admin pages been tested visually and interactively, not just behaviorally. The honest answer was no, because axe checks accessibility rather than aesthetics. A live interactive audit agent drove a real browser through every admin screen, captured forty-six screenshots at desktop and mobile, and exercised six end-to-end flows. The verdict was eight real defects including a bad admin URL falling through to the public marketing four-oh-four with public chrome rather than staying in the admin shell, the entire desktop Settings area showing raw ISO timestamps, and a mobile dashboard table overflow. A fix agent repaired the high and medium issues with before-after screenshot verification, including an admin-scoped not-found and error route plus a catch-all wildcard route because the App Router routes fully-unmatched segments to the root not-found, humanized timestamps reusing the existing formatter, a widened role select, and a mobile card reflow. A risk-tiered review board with parallel review agents across architecture, quality, testing, devops, and security returned go with two carry-forwards, and the blue-green cutover shipped the fixes, the cookie fix, the templates migration, the public and admin a11y improvements, and the responsive dashboard to production.
